18:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL uses three outer statuses: T, F, and C."}]
19:["$","p",null,{"children":"At first glance, this may look like another three-valued logic. That is partly true in form, but misleading in interpretation. The statuses are not unrestricted truth values. They are closure statuses."}]
1a:["$","p",null,{"children":"T means closure as acceptance."}]
1b:["$","p",null,{"children":"For the present institutional purpose, the claim may be treated as accepted. The phrase “present institutional purpose” matters. A model accepted for one narrow internal use is not automatically accepted for another public-facing use. A hiring model accepted for one role, one data version, and one review scope is not accepted for every role or future version."}]
1c:["$","p",null,{"children":"T is not eternal permission. It is scoped closure."}]
1d:["$","p",null,{"children":"F means closure as rejection."}]
1e:["$","p",null,{"children":"The current claim, version, or use can be closed as failed. This does not necessarily condemn a whole technology. A later version may return. A failed performance claim does not automatically settle a legal claim. F says that the present claim no longer stands for the current purpose."}]
1f:["$","p",null,{"children":"C means corrective non-closure."}]
20:["$","p",null,{"children":"This is the most important status. It means the claim is not ready for acceptance, but also not ready for final rejection. Something remains owed."}]
21:["$","p",null,{"children":"That “something” can vary. Evidence may be missing. Evidence may conflict. Audit records may be incomplete. Legal authority may be unsettled. Affected-party contestation may still be open. Data drift may require reassessment. A remedy process may be unfinished."}]
22:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL intentionally projects these different reasons into one closure status, C, because at the closure layer they have the same effect: the institution may not close the case."}]
23:["$","p",null,{"children":"But C is not meant to erase the difference between those reasons. In T-ACL, a C status must carry a diagnostic profile. 24:["$","p",null,{"children":"This is where ordinary “continued review” fails. Without ownership, deadline, and next action, continued review is not caution. It is delay."}]
25:["$","p",null,{"children":"In T-ACL, C is more like an institutional debt. It records that the institution still owes governance work before the case may be closed."}]

27:["$","p",null,{"children":"The core of T-ACL is not the three-status surface. It is certificate-controlled closure."}]
28:["$","p",null,{"children":"This is what distinguishes it from a simple traffic-light model."}]
29:["$","p",null,{"children":"In many governance documents, important claims appear lightly."}]
2a:["$","p",null,{"children":"The system is fair."}]
2b:["$","p",null,{"children":"The audit is complete."}]
2c:["$","p",null,{"children":"Legal risk is acceptable."}]
2d:["$","p",null,{"children":"Human review is sufficient."}]
2e:["$","p",null,{"children":"The appeal mechanism exists."}]
2f:["$","p",null,{"children":"The vendor’s evidence is reliable."}]
30:["$","p",null,{"children":"These statements may look like judgments. Sometimes they are only declarations. T-ACL does not allow a closure-critical declaration to enter as accepted or rejected merely because someone wrote it."}]
31:["$","p",null,{"children":"If a claim is going to support closure, it needs a status certificate."}]
32:["$","p",null,{"children":"A certificate is not a ceremonial approval stamp. It is a traceable record of the basis for the status. It should identify the evidence, method, competent authority, system or data version, uncertainty, reviewer independence, expiry condition, appeal path, and dissenting evidence where relevant."}]
33:["$","p",null,{"children":"Without an admissible certificate, the claim cannot enter as T or F. It falls back to C."}]
34:["$","p",null,{"children":"This rule has practical consequences."}]
35:["$","p",null,{"children":"A vendor cannot make a fairness claim closure-ready merely by saying it conducted fairness testing. A business unit cannot close a procedural-justice issue by writing that human review is available. A legal memo cannot convert a contested legal condition into acceptance merely by using the phrase “risk manageable.”"}]
36:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL does not require every detail to be public in the same way. Some materials may be confidential for legitimate reasons. Otherwise opacity becomes a path to approval."}]
37:["$","p",null,{"children":"This is the principle of no certificate, no closure."}]
38:["$","p",null,{"children":"Importantly, lack of a certificate does not always mean failure. Often it means C. The institution may supply evidence, complete review, or open a contestation process. But until then, the claim cannot support acceptance."}]
39:["$","p",null,{"children":"That is the point. High-risk AI systems often move forward not through complete absence of evidence, but through partial evidence, vendor evidence, internal self-certification, soft legal language, and procedural claims that are never tested. T-ACL does not need to declare all of those false. It asks whether they have earned a role in closure."}]
3a:["$","h2",null,{"id":"why-governance-needs-two-kinds-of-and","children":["$","a",null,{"href":"#why-governance-needs-two-kinds-of-and","children":"Why Governance Needs Two Kinds of “And”"}]}]
3b:["$","p",null,{"children":"Another design choice in T-ACL is the distinction between factual conjunction and deliberative conjunction."}]
3c:["$","p",null,{"children":"This may sound technical, but the practical problem is familiar."}]
3d:["$","p",null,{"children":"In ordinary factual reasoning, “A and B” behaves simply. If I say a dataset covers region A and region B, and it does not cover region B, the factual claim fails."}]
3e:["$","p",null,{"children":"Governance closure works differently."}]
3f:["$","p",null,{"children":"A deployment claim may depend on performance, fairness, legality, auditability, appeal, and remediation. When we say all of these matter, we are not merely conjoining facts. We are asking whether multiple governance dependencies are jointly ready for institutional closure."}]
40:["$","p",null,{"children":"Consider a system with two unresolved branches. A performance branch has already failed. A discrimination complaint is still being reviewed. Can the institution close the entire case as rejected because performance failed? In one sense the system should not be deployed. But if that rejection erases the pending discrimination review, affected-party evidence, or remedy obligations, the closure is too fast."}]
41:["$","p",null,{"children":"Now reverse the example. A competent authority has established a non-waivable legal block. The institution should not be able to keep the old deployment alive by saying that other audit questions remain under review. A hard stop should not be diluted into continued observation."}]
42:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL therefore separates factual conjunction from deliberative conjunction."}]
43:["$","p",null,{"children":"Factual conjunction handles ordinary co-satisfaction. Deliberative conjunction handles closure dependencies."}]
44:["$","p",null,{"children":"This distinction prevents two errors. An ordinary failed branch should not erase unresolved review duties. A validated legal, safety, or rights-based block should not be softened by the existence of other open issues."}]
45:["$","p",null,{"children":"This is why T-ACL includes ValidStop and Block."}]
46:["$","p",null,{"children":"ValidStop means that a stopping designation has been validated through authority, scope, evidentiary threshold, appeal path, revocation condition, and role separation. Block means that the validated stop propagates through the relevant deliberative closure path."}]
47:["$","p",null,{"children":"The important point is not that every negative finding becomes a block. The opposite is true. Blocking requires validation."}]
48:["$","p",null,{"children":"This prevents two abuses: uncertified acceptance and unvalidated blocking."}]
49:["$","p",null,{"children":"Uncertified acceptance lets systems pass too easily. Unvalidated blocking lets institutions stop, delay, or avoid review too easily. T-ACL constrains both."}]
4a:["$","h2",null,{"id":"the-fc-boundary-is-the-hard-part","children":["$","a",null,{"href":"#the-fc-boundary-is-the-hard-part","children":"The F/C Boundary Is the Hard Part"}]}]
4b:["$","p",null,{"children":"The most important boundary in T-ACL is not T versus F. It is F versus C."}]
4c:["$","p",null,{"children":"What does a negative finding mean?"}]
4d:["$","p",null,{"children":"AI governance often writes this too crudely."}]
4e:["$","p",null,{"children":"A fairness audit finds that a subgroup error rate is high. Does the system fail? It depends. The answer may require the use context, size of disparity, data quality, remediation window, legal standard, and affected-party evidence. 4f:["$","p",null,{"children":"A vendor cannot provide version history. Is that C or F? Again, it depends. Is version history necessary for the present decision? Is there alternative evidence? 50:["$","p",null,{"children":"A court finds that a public-sector risk system lacks adequate rights safeguards. That is not ordinary C. If the authority and scope are clear, the finding may support F with ValidStop."}]
51:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL’s distinction can be stated this way."}]
52:["$","p",null,{"children":"A remediable negative finding is C. It blocks acceptance but requires a diagnostic profile, owner, expiry, and next action."}]
53:["$","p",null,{"children":"A claim that can now be rejected is F. It may close the present claim as failed, but it does not necessarily block a larger decision or future version."}]
54:["$","p",null,{"children":"A non-waivable legal, safety, or rights failure is F plus ValidStop. In a deliberative closure path, it becomes blocking."}]
55:["$","p",null,{"children":"There is also an often ignored case: expired C. 58:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL is not another compliance framework."}]
59:["$","p",null,{"children":"It does not tell auditors which fairness metric to use. It does not tell lawyers how to interpret a statute. It does not define the correct ethical theory. It is not a model card, a bias-audit tool, a risk standard, or a court."}]
5a:["$","p",null,{"children":"Its position is between upstream judgment and downstream action."}]
5b:["$","p",null,{"children":"Upstream are the sources: technical evaluation, dataset documentation, fairness testing, legal analysis, ethics review, audit reports, affected-party evidence, court decisions, regulatory findings, institutional records."}]
5c:["$","p",null,{"children":"Downstream are the actions: approval, rejection, suspension, withdrawal, remediation, escalation, disclosure, appeal."}]
5d:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL asks how the upstream judgments support a closure status."}]
5e:["$","p",null,{"children":"This middle step is where many institutions are weak. They have inputs and actions, but the conversion from one to the other is vague. How does affected-party contestation change deployment eligibility?"}]
5f:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL requires these conversions to be recorded and constrained."}]
60:["$","p",null,{"children":"If a performance claim is T, it needs a certificate. If a legal issue is unresolved, it cannot quietly become T. If appeal is missing, the deployment claim should be C or, where appeal is non-waivable, blocked. If an audit record expires, the status must be reassessed."}]
61:["$","p",null,{"children":"It does not replace judgment. It prevents substitution of judgment with vague language."}]

74:["$","p",null,{"children":"An argumentation system may leave an equity claim undecided. T-ACL asks: does that undecided output put the deployment claim into C? What is the diagnostic profile? Who owns the next action? When does it expire?"}]

77:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL does not replace assurance cases, argumentation frameworks, workflow systems, legal reasoning, or audit tools. It translates their outputs into a closure interface and tries to prevent that translation from being captured by vague managerial language."}]

79:["$","h2",null,{"id":"what-t-acl-cannot-do","children":["$","a",null,{"href":"#what-t-acl-cannot-do","children":"What T-ACL Cannot Do"}]}]
7a:["$","p",null,{"children":"The limits matter."}]
7b:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL cannot guarantee that certificates are truthful. An institution can produce polished but misleading certificates. It can disclose selectively. It can simulate independence while the review is captured. These are political, legal, and institutional problems. T-ACL can expose where certificates are being used, but it cannot make them honest by itself."}]
7c:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL cannot decide what fairness requires. That depends on technical measures, law, ethics, and context. It only requires that a fairness judgment used for closure have evidence, method, authority, uncertainty, and contestability."}]
7d:["$","p",null,{"children":"It cannot decide what the law is. Courts, regulators, lawyers, and public procedures do that. T-ACL records how a legal status affects closure."}]
7e:["$","p",null,{"children":"It also cannot validate itself through three public cases. Those cases show representational adequacy, not institutional effectiveness. To test effectiveness, one would need implementation, organizational use, comparison with ordinary approval workflows, affected-party evidence, and internal records."}]
7f:["$","p",null,{"children":"These limits are not afterthoughts. They define the framework. If T-ACL is presented as a universal governance engine, it becomes implausible. Its best role is as a certificate-gated closure-status interface."}]
80:["$","p",null,{"children":"Why Formalize Common Sense?"}]
81:["$","p",null,{"children":"A fair objection is that much of this is common sense."}]

84:["$","p",null,{"children":"T-ACL formalizes common sense."}]
85:["$","p",null,{"children":"But institutional failures often persist because common sense is not embedded in procedure."}]
86:["$","p",null,{"children":"Everyone knows continued monitoring should not last forever. Yet it does. Everyone knows vendors should not simply certify themselves. Everyone knows legal blocks should not be softened into managerial risk language. Yet project documents often do exactly that."}]

8b:["$","p",null,{"children":"Once a valid hard stop exists, it cannot be diluted into ordinary observation."}]
